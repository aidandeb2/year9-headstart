  1. Welcome to Headstart algebra. This is Lesson 4: dividing algebraic terms. By the end of this video, you'll be able to simplify expressions by dividing terms.
  2. First, division and fractions. A division can be written as a fraction. So x ÷ 7 is the same as x/7.
  3. Anything divided by itself is one. So x/x is 1. In the same way, a/a is 1, and b/b is 1.
  4. We'll also use the highest common factor, or H C F. That's the biggest number that divides into both numbers. The H C F of 6 and 3 is 3.
  5. Here's how to divide algebraic terms. We'll simplify (6a⁵b³)/(3a³b). There are four steps.
  6. Step one: write each term in expanded form. On the top, that's 6 times five a's times three b's. On the bottom, it's 3 times three a's times one b.
  7. Step two: divide the coefficients by their H C F. The H C F of 6 and 3 is 3. So 6 ÷ 3 is 2, and the bottom becomes 1.
  8. Step three: cancel the letters that are on both the top and the bottom. Three a's cancel, leaving two a's on top. One b cancels, leaving two b's on top.
  9. Step four: write what's left in index form. The answer is 2a²b².
  10. There's a quicker way: the index law. Divide the coefficients, and subtract the indices of matching letters, top minus bottom.
  11. For example, (a⁵)/(a³). The indices subtract: 5 − 3 is 2. So the answer is a².
  12. If the index on the top and the bottom is the same, they cancel completely. (a²)/(a²) is 1.
  13. And if the bigger index is on the bottom, the leftover stays on the bottom. (a²)/(a⁵) is 1/(a³).
  14. These divisibility rules help you find the H C F. A number is divisible by 2 if it is even.
  15. It's divisible by 3 if its digits add to a multiple of 3. For example, 12 is divisible by 3, because 1 plus 2 is 3.
  16. And it's divisible by 5 if it ends in 0 or 5.
  17. Sometimes you need to divide by a fraction. Let's simplify (3x)/8 ÷ (5x)/4.
  18. To divide, multiply by the reciprocal. That means flip the second fraction upside down, and change the divide sign to times.
  19. Now multiply the tops, and multiply the bottoms. We get (12x)/(40x).
  20. Finally, cancel the x, and divide the top and bottom by their H C F, which is 4. The answer is 3/10.
  21. Three helpful hints. First, count carefully when you write a letter with a big index in expanded form.
  22. Second, if everything cancels, the answer is 1, not 0. And if the sign is negative, keep it with the coefficient.
  23. And third, simplify first, and then substitute. It's much quicker than substituting into the big fraction.
  24. You should now be able to write a division as a fraction, divide the coefficients by their H C F, cancel letters on the top and bottom, use the index law, and simplify first before substituting. Next, watch the worked examples video, then try the questions in your booklet.

  1. In this video we'll work through six worked examples from Lesson 4. For each one, pause the video and try the problem yourself first. Then follow the think and write steps to check your thinking.
  2. Example one: cancelling a pronumeral. Simplify (5a²)/a.
  3. Write the top in expanded form: 5 × a × a, over a.
  4. Cancel one a from the top and the bottom, because a/a is 1. That leaves 5 × a.
  5. Write it in index form. The answer is 5a.
  6. Example two: using the index law. Simplify (8a³b)/(2a²b²).
  7. First divide the coefficients. 8 ÷ 2 is 4.
  8. Now subtract the indices, top minus bottom. For a, that's 3 − 2, which is 1, so one a stays on top. For b, it's 1 − 2, which is −1, so one b is left on the bottom.
  9. Put it together. The answer is (4a)/b.
  10. Example three: using the H C F of the coefficients. Simplify (12x⁵y²)/(8x²y).
  11. The H C F of 12 and 8 is 4. Divide both by 4. 12 ÷ 4 is 3, and 8 ÷ 4 is 2.
  12. For x, 5 − 2 is 3, and it stays on top. For y, 2 − 1 is 1, and it stays on top.
  13. So the answer is (3x³y)/2.
  14. Example four: a divide sign, and negatives. Simplify 30a³b² ÷ −6ab².
  15. Divide the coefficients. 30 ÷ −6 is −5, because different signs give a negative.
  16. For a, 3 − 1 is 2. For b, 2 − 2 is 0, so the b's cancel completely.
  17. The answer is −5a².
  18. Example five: multiply on top first. Simplify (x⁴ × x³)/(x²).
  19. On the top we're multiplying, so add the indices. 4 + 3 is 7. That gives (x⁷)/(x²).
  20. Now we divide, so subtract the indices. 7 − 2 is 5. The answer is x⁵.
  21. Example six: simplify, then substitute. Simplify (12a³b)/(4a), then evaluate when a = 2 and b = 5.
  22. 12 ÷ 4 is 3. For a, 3 − 1 is 2. The b stays. So we get 3a²b.
  23. Now substitute. 3 × 2² × 5 is 3 × 4 × 5, which is 60. Simplifying first made that much quicker.
  24. That's the end of the six worked examples. Now try the questions in your lesson booklet, and remember to show your working in the boxes. If everything cancels, the answer is 1, not 0.

The big idea

Divide the coefficients. Subtract the indices.
  1. Write the division as a fraction.
  2. Divide the coefficients by their HCF.
  3. Subtract the indices: top minus bottom. A leftover on the bottom stays on the bottom.
  4. Write it back in index form.
Example: Simplify 30a3b2 ÷ −6ab2
Coefficients30 ÷ −6 = −5 (different signs give a negative)
Subtract the indicesa: 3 − 1 = 2 · b: 2 − 2 = 0 (they cancel)
Write it back−5a2
Remember: If everything cancels, the answer is 1, not 0. And simplify first, then substitute. It's much quicker.
