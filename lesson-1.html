  1. Welcome to Headstart algebra. This is Lesson 1: adding and subtracting algebraic terms. By the end of this video, you'll be able to identify like terms, and simplify expressions by collecting them.
  2. First, some words to know. Here's an expression: seven x, minus four y, plus nine. Let's use it to meet five important words.
  3. A pronumeral is a letter that stands for a number. In this expression, the pronumerals are x and y.
  4. A term is one part of an expression. Terms are separated by plus and minus signs, and the sign in front belongs to the term. So this expression has three terms: seven x, minus four y, and plus nine.
  5. The coefficient is the number multiplying the pronumeral. In seven x, the coefficient is seven. In minus four y, it's minus four. And x on its own means one x, so its coefficient is one.
  6. A constant is a term with no pronumeral, just a number. Here, the constant is nine.
  7. And an expression is terms joined by plus and minus signs, like this whole thing.
  8. The laws of arithmetic still work in algebra. The commutative law says that order doesn't matter when we add or multiply. So a plus b equals b plus a, and a times b equals b times a. This means xy and yx are the same thing. But be careful: order does matter for subtraction. Five minus two is not the same as two minus five.
  9. The associative law says that grouping doesn't matter for adding or multiplying either. We can add a and b first, or b and c first, and get the same answer.
  10. Now, like terms. Like terms have exactly the same pronumerals, with exactly the same powers. Only the coefficients can be different.
  11. Five x and eight x are like terms. They both have just x.
  12. Five x and five y are not like terms, because the letters are different.
  13. The order of the letters can change. xy and yx are like terms.
  14. To check powers, write them in expanded form. a squared b is a times a times b, but a b squared is a times b times b. So they are not like terms.
  15. Now let's simplify an expression by collecting like terms. There are three steps. We'll use this example: four x, plus three y, plus two x, minus y.
  16. Step one: circle or underline the like terms. The x terms are four x and two x. The y terms are three y and minus y.
  17. Step two: group them together. Each sign goes along with its term.
  18. Step three: add and subtract the coefficients. Four plus two is six, and three minus one is two. So the answer is six x plus two y.
  19. Here are two helpful hints. First, the sign in front of a term belongs to that term. If you move the term, take its sign with it. For example, x plus seven y, minus three x, plus five y, can be regrouped as x minus three x, plus seven y plus five y.
  20. Second, x on its own means one x. And unlike terms can't be combined. Three x plus five is already as simple as it gets, because an x term and a constant are unlike.
  21. You should now be able to name the parts of an expression, decide whether two terms are like terms, collect like terms while keeping each sign with its term, and check your answer by substituting a value. Next, watch the worked examples video, then try the questions in your booklet.

  4. Next group: three y and y both have just y.

  6. Example two: are they like terms? Part a: xy and yx. Part b: x squared and x. Part c: a squared b and a b squared.
  7. For part a, letters can swap places because of the commutative law. xy equals yx, so yes, they're like terms.
  8. For part b, expand the powers. x squared is x times x, but x is just x. So no.
  9. For part c, expand again. a squared b is a times a times b, but a b squared is a times b times b. So no.
  10. Example three: collect like terms. Simplify ten x, minus three x, plus three, plus x.
  11. Circle the like terms, and remember that x means one x. The x terms are ten x, minus three x and plus x. The constant is three.
  12. Group them, taking each sign along: ten x minus three x plus x, plus three.
  13. Now add and subtract the coefficients: ten minus three plus one equals eight. So the answer is eight x plus three. Eight x and three are unlike terms, so we stop.
  14. Example four: letters in a different order. Simplify four xy, plus two x, minus three yx, minus x.
  15. xy and yx are like terms, and two x and x are like terms. Group them: four xy minus three yx, plus two x minus x.
  16. Four minus three equals one, and two minus one equals one. So the answer is xy plus x.
  17. Example five: terms with powers. Simplify three x squared y, plus two xy squared, minus x squared y, plus four xy squared.
  18. Expand to see which are alike. x squared y is x times x times y. xy squared is x times y times y.
  19. Group the x squared y terms, and the xy squared terms.
  20. Three minus one equals two, and two plus four equals six. So the answer is two x squared y, plus six xy squared.

  22. Collect like terms. Five a plus three a is eight a. Two b minus b is b. So the answer is eight a plus b.
  23. Now substitute into the original expression. Five times two, plus two times three, plus three times two, minus three. That's ten plus six plus six minus three, which equals nineteen.

The big idea

Like terms have exactly the same letters with exactly the same powers
  1. Circle or underline the like terms.
  2. Group them together. Each sign travels with its term.
  3. Add and subtract the coefficients.
Example: Simplify 4x + 3y + 2x − y
Circle the like termsx terms: 4x and 2x · y terms: 3y and −y
Group them4x + 2x + 3y − y
Add the coefficients6x + 2y
Remember: x on its own means 1x. And 6x + 2y is as simple as it gets, because x terms and y terms are unlike.

Spot the mistake Click each one to check your thinking

2x + 3y = 5xyWhat went wrong? ▾
Already as simple as it gets: 2x + 3y = 2x + 3y

Unlike terms can't be combined. This one is already as simple as it gets.

5m2 + 2n + m2 = 6m + 2nWhat went wrong? ▾
Fixed: 5m2 + 2n + m2 = 6m2 + 2n

Adding changes the coefficient only. The power stays exactly the same.

9x − x = 9What went wrong? ▾
Fixed: 9x − x = 8x

A lonely x means 1x. So 9 − 1 = 8, and the x stays.
