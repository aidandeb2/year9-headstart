  1. Welcome to Headstart algebra. This is Lesson 3, the checkpoint. Today we revise adding, subtracting and multiplying terms, so you can check you're confident before moving on.
  2. First, the parts of an expression. Here's an expression: 4x − 3 + 9y − 4xy². The terms are separated by plus and minus signs. So the terms are 4x, −3, 9y, and −4xy².
  3. The pronumerals are the letters. In this expression they are x and y.
  4. The coefficient is the number multiplying the letters. Here we have 4, 9, and −4. Notice the sign belongs to the term.
  5. The constant is the term with no letters, just a number. Here it is −3.
  6. And the highest index is the biggest power on a letter. For y, the highest index is 2, from the term −4xy².
  7. Now, a quick revision of Lesson 1. To add and subtract, we collect like terms. Take 5x + 2y − 3x + 4y + 7.
  8. Step one: circle the like terms. The x terms are 5x and −3x. The y terms are 2y and 4y.
  9. Step two: group them together, taking each sign with its term.
  10. Step three: add and subtract the coefficients. 5 − 3 is 2, and 2 + 4 is 6. The constant, 7, stays on its own. The answer is 2x + 6y + 7. This works exactly the same way with decimals and fractions.
  11. And a quick revision of Lesson 2. To multiply terms, take 3a²b × −4ab³.
  12. Step one: multiply the coefficients, and check the sign. 3 × −4 is −12, because the signs are different.
  13. Step two: add the indices of matching letters. For a, that's 2 + 1, which is 3. For b, it's 1 + 3, which is 4.
  14. Step three: write it as the coefficient first, then the letters in alphabetical order. The answer is −12a³b⁴.
  15. The most common mistake is mixing up adding and multiplying. 3x + 4x is 7x. Only the coefficient changes.
  16. But 3x × 4x is 12x². Here the coefficients multiply, and the indices add.
  17. Another way to see it: x + x is 2x, but x × x is x². If you're not sure, try a number for x and check.
  18. Here are three hints for the checkpoint. First, remember the words: coefficient is the number multiplying, constant is a number on its own, and pronumeral is the letter.
  19. Second, the highest index is the biggest power on that letter, in any term.
  20. And third, look at the sign in front of each term before you add, subtract or multiply.
  21. Now it's your turn. Finish any questions you missed in Lessons 1 and 2, then do the checkpoint questions, and use the catch-up tracker in your booklet. When you're ready, try the quiz on this website.

The big idea

Adding and multiplying are different jobs
  1. Adding or subtracting like terms changes the coefficient only.
  2. Multiplying terms multiplies the coefficients and adds the indices.
  3. Not sure? Try a number for x, such as x = 2, and check both sides.
