  1. Welcome to Headstart algebra. This is Lesson 7, the review. We'll bring together everything you've learned: collecting, multiplying, dividing, expanding and factorising.
  2. Here's a summary of the five skills. Let's go through them one at a time.
  3. To collect like terms, look for the same letters with the same powers, and add or subtract the coefficients.
  4. To multiply, multiply the coefficients and add the indices of matching letters.
  5. To divide, divide the coefficients by their H C F, and subtract the indices. Then cancel.
  6. To expand, multiply every term inside the bracket by the term outside.
  7. And to factorise, take out the H C F, and divide every term by it.
  8. Now, some common mistakes to watch for. First, don't mix up adding and multiplying. 3x + 4x is 7x, but 3x × 4x is 12x².
  9. Second, unlike terms can't be combined. 2x + 3y cannot be simplified.
  10. Third, a minus sign in front of a bracket flips every sign inside. −(a + 2) is −a − 2.
  11. And fourth, when you factorise, take out the highest common factor, and divide every term by it. A term that is all H C F leaves a 1 behind.
  12. How do you choose the right skill? Look at the expression. If the same letters and powers are being added or subtracted, collect like terms.
  13. If terms are being multiplied, add the indices.
  14. If terms are being divided, subtract the indices.
  15. If there's a bracket with a term outside, expand.
  16. And if you have a sum with a common factor, factorise.
  17. Expanding and factorising are opposites, so you can use one to check the other. Here, −7p(3q − 4) expands to −21pq + 28p.
  18. And 15a³bc + 9a²c factorises to 3a²c(5ab + 3).
  19. To check a factorised answer, expand it. 3a²c(5ab + 3) takes you straight back to the original expression. You can also check any answer by substituting a number.

  1. In this video we'll work through five review examples. For each one, decide which skill you need, then pause the video and try it yourself. Then follow the think and write steps to check your thinking.
  2. Example one: which skill? Look at −7p(3q − 4).
  3. There's a bracket with a term outside, so we need to expand. Multiply each term inside by −7p.
  4. −7p × 3q is −21pq. And −7p × −4 is 28p, because two negatives make a positive. The answer is −21pq + 28p.
  5. Example two: which skill? Look at 15a³bc + 9a²c.
  6. This is a sum with a common factor, so we factorise. The H C F is 3a²c, so write that in front of a bracket.
  7. Divide each term by 3a²c. 15a³bc ÷ 3a²c is 5ab, and 9a²c ÷ 3a²c is 3.
  8. So the answer is 3a²c(5ab + 3). To check, expand it. 3a²c × 5ab is 15a³bc, and 3a²c × 3 is 9a²c. We're back to the original.
  9. Example three: spot and fix the mistake. Alex wrote −(a + 2) = −a + 2. What went wrong?
  10. The expression −(a + 2) means −1 × (a + 2). So we multiply both terms by −1.
  11. −1 × a is −a, and −1 × 2 is −2. The minus flips every sign inside the bracket, not just the first one. The correct answer is −a − 2.
  12. Example four: expand, then collect. Simplify 4(x + 2) − (x − 3).
  13. Expand the first bracket. 4(x + 2) is 4x + 8.
  14. Now the second bracket. −(x − 3) flips every sign, so it becomes −x + 3.
  15. Collect like terms. 4x − x is 3x, and 8 + 3 is 11. The answer is 3x + 11.
  16. Example five: a real-world problem. A rectangle has width x centimetres, and area x² + 6x square centimetres. Find its length.
  17. Area is length times width, so the length is the area divided by the width. We can find this by factorising the area. The H C F of x² and 6x is x. So x² + 6x is x(x + 6).
  18. The width is x, so the other factor is the length. The length is x + 6 centimetres.
  19. That's the end of the review examples. Now finish your catch-up tracker, and try the review questions in your booklet. Remember to choose the right skill first, and check your answers.

The big idea

Pick the right tool for the job
  1. Look at the expression. Is there a bracket? Expand. Is it a sum you can pull a factor out of? Factorise.
  2. Same letters and powers being added? Collect like terms.
  3. Multiplying or dividing terms? Use the index laws.
  4. Check your work by substituting a number, or by doing the opposite operation.

Not wrong, but not finished. Take out the highest common factor, which is 4.
