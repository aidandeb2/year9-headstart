  1. Welcome to Headstart algebra. This is Lesson 6: factorising. By the end of this video, you'll be able to find the H C F of algebraic terms, and factorise expressions by taking out the H C F.
  2. First, factors. A factor divides exactly into a term. A common factor divides exactly into every term in a set.
  3. The highest common factor, or H C F, is the product of all the common factors. It can have a number part, and a pronumeral part.
  4. Here are some examples. The H C F of x and 7x is x.
  5. The H C F of 6m, 12mn and 8mp is 2m. The number 2 divides into 6, 12 and 8, and m is in all three terms.
  6. And the H C F of 15a³bc and 9a²c is 3a²c. Let's see how to find that.
  7. To find the H C F, follow three steps. We'll use 15a³bc and 9a²c.
  8. Step one: write each term as a product of prime and algebraic factors. 15a³bc is 3 × 5 × a × a × a × b × c. And 9a²c is 3 × 3 × a × a × c.
  9. Step two: circle the common factors. Both terms have a 3, two a's, and a c.
  10. Step three: multiply them. 3 × a × a × c is 3a²c. That's the H C F.
  11. Factorising is the opposite of expanding. When we expand, we remove the brackets: 7(a + 2) becomes 7a + 14.
  12. When we factorise, we put the brackets back: 7a + 14 becomes 7(a + 2).
  13. Now let's factorise 8x + 28. There are four steps.
  14. Step one: identify the H C F of all the terms. The H C F of 8 and 28 is 4.
  15. Step two: write the H C F in front of a bracket.
  16. Step three: inside the bracket, divide each term by the H C F. 8x ÷ 4 is 2x, and 28 ÷ 4 is 7.
  17. Step four: simplify the bracket. The answer is 4(2x + 7). And you can always check by expanding. 4 × 2x is 8x, and 4 × 7 is 28. We get back to where we started.
  18. Sometimes it's best to take out a negative H C F, especially when the first term is negative. For −4a − 12, we take out −4. −4a ÷ −4 is a, and −12 ÷ −4 is plus 3. So we get −4(a + 3).
  19. Check by expanding: −4 × a is −4a, and −4 × 3 is −12. It matches.
  20. Three helpful hints. First, look at the coefficients and all the letters when you look for the H C F.
  21. Second, take out the highest common factor. If you only take out part of it, you must factorise again. 4a + 8 = 2(2a + 4) is not finished, but 4(a + 2) is.
  22. And third, the number of terms inside the bracket must match the number of terms you started with.
  23. You should now be able to write a term as a product of its factors, find the H C F of terms, factorise by taking out the H C F, factorise with indices and with a negative H C F, and check by expanding. Next, watch the worked examples video, then try the questions in your booklet.

  1. In this video we'll work through five worked examples from Lesson 6. For each one, pause the video and try the problem yourself first. Then follow the think and write steps to check your thinking.
  2. Example one: identifying the H C F. Find the H C F of 2a and 5a, and of 12xy and −18x³.
  3. For part a, write each term as a product of prime and algebraic factors. 2a is 2 × a, and 5a is 5 × a.
  4. The only common factor is a. So the H C F is a.
  5. For part b, write each as a product. A negative can be written as times minus one. 12xy is 2 × 2 × 3 × x × y. And −18x³ is −1 × 2 × 3 × 3 × x × x × x.
  6. Multiply the common factors: 2, 3, and x. The H C F is 6x.
  7. Example two: factorising. Factorise 8x + 28 and 9xyz − 12wxy.
  8. For part a, the H C F of 8 and 28 is 4. Divide each term by 4. 8x ÷ 4 is 2x, and 28 ÷ 4 is 7. So we get 4(2x + 7).
  9. For part b, the H C F of 9xyz and 12wxy is 3xy. Write that outside a bracket.
  10. Divide each term by 3xy. 9xyz ÷ 3xy is 3z, and 12wxy ÷ 3xy is 4w. The minus sign stays. So the answer is 3xy(3z − 4w).
  11. Example three: terms with indices. Factorise a² − 6a and 10m³n + 15m²n².
  12. For part a, a² is a × a, so the H C F is a. Divide each term by a. We get a(a − 6).
  13. For part b, the H C F of 10 and 15 is 5. The lowest power of m is m², and the lowest power of n is n. So the H C F is 5m²n.
  14. Divide each term by 5m²n. 10m³n ÷ 5m²n is 2m, and 15m²n² ÷ 5m²n is 3n.
  15. The answer is 5m²n(2m + 3n).
  16. Example four: a negative H C F. Factorise −4a − 12.
  17. Take out −4, the H C F with a negative sign.
  18. Divide each term by −4. −4a ÷ −4 is a, and −12 ÷ −4 is plus 3. So we get −4(a + 3).
  19. Check by expanding. −4 × a is −4a, and −4 × 3 is −12. It matches the original.
  20. Example five: a composite shape. A rectangle is 5x wide and 3y high. A triangle sits on top, with base 5x and height 2x. Find the area, then factorise it.
  21. The rectangle's area is length times width. 5x × 3y is 15xy.
  22. The triangle's area is half times base times height. ½ × 5x × 2x is 5x².
  23. The total area is 15xy + 5x². The H C F is 5x. So we factorise it as 5x(3y + x).
  24. That's the end of the five worked examples. Now try the questions in your lesson booklet, and remember to check each answer by expanding it.

The big idea

Factorising is expanding backwards
  1. Find the HCF of all the terms (numbers and pronumerals).
  2. Write the HCF in front of a bracket.
  3. Inside the bracket, divide each term by the HCF.
  4. Check by expanding. You should get the original back.
Example: Factorise 15a3bc + 9a2c
Write as factors15a3bc = 3 × 5 × a × a × a × b × c · 9a2c = 3 × 3 × a × a × c
Multiply the common factorsHCF = 3 × a × a × c = 3a2c
Divide each term by the HCF3a2c(5ab + 3)
Remember: Take out the HIGHEST common factor: 4a + 8 = 2(2a + 4) isn't finished, but 4(a + 2) is.
