Algebra glossary

Every key word from the seven lessons.

Area modelLesson 5

Drawing a rectangle to show why expanding works. The area of the whole equals the sum of the smaller areas.

10(a + 3) = 10a + 30

Associative lawLesson 1

Grouping doesn't matter when you add or multiply.

(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)

BaseLesson 2

The number or pronumeral that is being multiplied by itself.

In a3 the base is a

CancelLesson 4

Divide the top and bottom of a fraction by the same thing. Pronumerals on the top and bottom cancel to 1.

6xy3x = 2y

CoefficientLesson 1

The number multiplying the pronumeral(s) in a term. A lonely x means 1x, and −y means −1y.

In 5x it is 5. In −y it is −1. In x it is 1.

Commutative lawLesson 1

Order doesn't matter when you add or multiply (but it does matter for subtraction and division).

a + b = b + a and xy = yx

ConstantLesson 1

A term with no pronumeral, just a number.

In 7x − 4y + 9 the constant is 9

Distributive lawLesson 5

The term outside the bracket is shared with every term inside.

a(b + c) = ab + ac

Division as a fractionLesson 4

A division can be written as a fraction. Anything divided by itself is 1.

x ÷ 7 = x7 and xx = 1

EvaluateLesson 1

Find the numerical value of an expression after substituting.

Evaluate 3x + 1 when x = 4: 3(4) + 1 = 13

ExpandLesson 5

Remove the brackets by multiplying every term inside by the term outside.

3(x + 4) = 3x + 12

Expanded formLesson 2

Every factor written out in full.

3a3b = 3 × a × a × a × b

ExpressionLesson 1

Terms joined together by + and − signs.

7x − 4y + 9

FactorLesson 6

A number or term that divides exactly into another.

3 and 5 are factors of 15

FactoriseLesson 6

Put the brackets back. Take the HCF out the front and divide every term by it. It is the opposite of expanding.

8x + 28 = 4(2x + 7)

Highest common factor (HCF)Lesson 4

The biggest factor that divides exactly into every term. It can have a number part and a pronumeral part.

The HCF of 15a3bc and 9a2c is 3a2c

Index (power)Lesson 2

The small raised number. It tells you how many times the base is multiplied by itself. A pronumeral with no index has an index of 1.

a3 = a × a × a (index 3, base a)

Index formLesson 2

Repeated multiplication written with an index.

2 × x × x × y × y × y = 2x2y3

Index law (dividing)Lesson 4

When you divide terms, divide the coefficients and subtract the indices (top minus bottom).

a5 ÷ a3 = a2

Index law (multiplying)Lesson 2

When you multiply terms, multiply the coefficients and add the indices of matching pronumerals.

a3 × a4 = a7

Like termsLesson 1

Terms with exactly the same pronumerals and exactly the same powers. Only the coefficients can be different. The order of the letters doesn't matter.

5x and 8x, or xy and yx

PronumeralLesson 1

A letter that stands for a number.

x, y, a, b

ReciprocalLesson 4

A fraction flipped upside down. To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal.

The reciprocal of x8 is 8x

SimplifyLesson 1

Write an expression in its shortest form, for example by collecting like terms.

4x + 3y + 2x − y = 6x + 2y

SubstituteLesson 1

Replace each pronumeral with a number. Substituting is a great way to check an answer.

If a = 2, then 8a + 3 = 8(2) + 3 = 19

TermLesson 1

One part of an expression. Terms are separated by + and − signs, and the sign in front belongs to the term.

7x − 4y + 9 has 3 terms: 7x, −4y and +9

Unlike termsLesson 1

Terms that are not like terms. They can't be added or subtracted into a single term.

x and x2, or a2b and ab2
